Executive Summary

This was a rough week. All 12 major funds and all 11 sectors fell, SPY lost -8.6% and QQQ -10.5%, among their worst weeks since 2008, and the VIX nearly doubled from 19.3 to 35.9, its highest close of the past year. SPY (-11.5% from its 52-week high), the Dow (-11.4%) and the Russell 2000 (-15.9%) all entered a correction this week, and QQQ is now -19.9% from its high, a tenth of a point from a bear market. Tech is already in one, -26.2% from its high after a -12.5% week and -19.6% month, and communication services closed +0.1% above its past-year low. The year's pattern held, and the gap got wider. Consumer staples fell only -2.0% and are still up +18.7% this year; value is up +13.9% while growth is down -6.6%. People ran to Treasuries and the dollar, not gold or crypto. The 10-year yield fell -23 bps to 4.92%, and the dollar ended within 0.1% of its past-year high. Gold dropped -8.9%, its second-worst week in 10 years, and closed at its past-year low, as did silver and platinum. Bitcoin fell -13.6% to end +0.7% above its past-year low, and BNB, XRP, Dogecoin, Cardano and Litecoin closed at past-year lows. The high-yield spread widened to 3.52%, above its 2.90% 1-year average. Keep an eye on the VIX. At 35.9 it is in the method's High zone, historically one of its most reliable entry windows, and it now sits above the 3-month VIX. Individual investors got less bearish (42.2%, from 51.4%), but that survey closed on Wednesday, so next week's reading will say more.

TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities) pay a yield on top of inflation, a “real” yield. The 10-year breakeven rate (10-year Treasury minus 10-year TIPS, same day) is 2.15% on Nov 20, 2026: roughly the yearly inflation the bond market expects. Muni (MUB) is the iShares National Muni Bond ETF: its yield as of the fetch, and its total return over each period in bps (+100 bps = +1%), green when its price rose. The yield curve rows are the gap between two yields in points; below zero means inverted. Real Fed Funds is the Fed funds rate minus CPI inflation over 12 months. Changes are red when a yield or rate rose (a rising yield means a falling bond price) and green when it fell. Status is Inverted (red) when a yield is below a shorter maturity's, or a gap is below zero, and that comes first. Otherwise it is the move over the past month: Rising (red) or Falling (green) at 10 bps or more, otherwise Steady. The yield curve rows are not colored: a gap is Steepening when it widened by 10 bps or more over the past month, Flattening when it narrowed by that much, otherwise Steady; Real Fed Funds is Behind (red) below zero, when CPI inflation is above the Fed funds rate. GDP Growth is real GDP's growth in its latest quarter at an annual pace; it is the other way round: a rise, and Rising, are green, a fall red. Its 1M column compares it with the quarter before. Unemployment is the share of the labor force without a job and looking for one, monthly. Mortgage rates are Freddie Mac's weekly survey averages; credit card (accounts paying interest) and car loan rates are the Fed's quarterly survey of banks.

Treasury and TIPS yields are the U.S. Treasury's daily yield curves (home.treasury.gov) at this Friday's close, with their history from FRED; the other rates from FRED, including CPI and unemployment (Bureau of Labor Statistics, UNRATE) and PCE and GDP growth (Bureau of Economic Analysis, A191RL1Q225SBEA).
