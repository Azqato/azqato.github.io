This Week

Executive Summary

This was a rough week. All 12 major funds and all 11 sectors fell, SPY lost 8.6% and QQQ 10.5%, and the VIX nearly doubled from 19.3 to 35.9, its highest close of the past year. Tech took the worst of it, down 12.5% this week and 19.6% over the month. The year's pattern held, and the gap got wider. Consumer staples fell only 2.0% and are still up 18.7% this year, and the value fund is up 13.9% while the growth fund is now down 6.6%. Equal weight, dividend and small-cap value funds all fell less than the S&P 500. Treasuries were the place people ran to, not gold or crypto. The 10-year yield fell 23 bps to 4.92%, and over 20 days stocks trailed Treasuries by 9.8 points. Gold dropped 8.9% and Bitcoin 13.6%, and the high-yield spread widened to 3.52%, above its 2.90% 1-year average. Keep an eye on the VIX. At 35.9 it is in the method's High zone, which it calls historically one of the most reliable entry windows, and it now sits above the 3-month VIX. Individual investors actually got less bearish (42.2%, from 51.4%), but that survey closed on Wednesday, before the week was done, so next week's reading will say more.

TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities) pay a yield on top of inflation, a “real” yield. The 10-year breakeven rate (10-year Treasury minus 10-year TIPS, same day) is 2.15% on Nov 20, 2026: roughly the yearly inflation the bond market expects. Muni (MUB) is the iShares National Muni Bond ETF: its yield as of the fetch, and its total return over each period in bps (+100 bps = +1%), green when its price rose. The yield curve rows are the gap between two yields in points; below zero means inverted. Real Fed Funds is the Fed funds rate minus CPI inflation over 12 months. Changes are red when a yield or rate rose (a rising yield means a falling bond price) and green when it fell. Status is Inverted (red) when a yield is below a shorter maturity's, or a gap is below zero, and that comes first. Otherwise it is the move over the past month: Rising (red) or Falling (green) at 10 bps or more, otherwise Steady. The yield curve rows are not colored: a gap is Steepening when it widened by 10 bps or more over the past month, Flattening when it narrowed by that much, otherwise Steady; Real Fed Funds is Behind (red) below zero, when CPI inflation is above the Fed funds rate. Mortgage rates are Freddie Mac's weekly survey averages; credit card (accounts paying interest) and car loan rates are the Fed's quarterly survey of banks.
