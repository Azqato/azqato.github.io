This Week

Executive Summary

This was a relief week. Every major fund and every sector finished higher, and the VIX dropped from 25.6 to 19.3. Still, one good week doesn't erase a rough month: QQQ is down 8.6% and tech is down 13.2% since mid-October. Zoom out to the year and the story is value and defensive stocks, not growth. Health care, staples and energy have carried the market while the growth fund is up less than 4%. The question now is whether tech's bounce sticks or fades. Inflation is the thing to watch. CPI is running at 4.1% and rising, which is now above the 3.88% Fed funds rate, and the 2-year yield has climbed 139 bps this year. The next CPI report and how the Fed responds will set the tone, with 30-year mortgages already at 7.11%. Individual investors aren't buying the rally yet. Bearish sentiment hit 51.4%, and bears have outnumbered bulls for four weeks straight. If that climbs past 60% it reaches the method's Strong level.

The yield curve rows are the gap between two yields in points; below zero means inverted. Real Fed Funds is the Fed funds rate minus CPI inflation over 12 months. Changes are red when a yield or rate rose (a rising yield means a falling bond price) and green when it fell. Status is Inverted (red) when a yield is below a shorter maturity's, or a gap is below zero, and that comes first. Otherwise it is the move over the past month: Rising (red) or Falling (green) at 10 bps or more, otherwise Steady. The yield curve rows are not colored: a gap is Steepening when it widened by 10 bps or more over the past month, Flattening when it narrowed by that much, otherwise Steady; Real Fed Funds is Behind (red) below zero, when CPI inflation is above the Fed funds rate.
