- [ ] 46. **h2** [main]: What Finviz Is For
- [ ] 47. **paragraph** [main]: Finviz is a stock screener, not a portfolio tracker. Its purpose in this methodology is different from Seeking Alpha's. Finviz answers the question: "Which companies across the entire market currently look like candidates worth evaluating?" Seeking Alpha then answers: "Do the full 12 metrics on this specific company confirm that it is worth holding?"
- [ ] 48. **paragraph** [main]: Finviz does not perfectly replicate every column from the Seeking Alpha watchlist. It does not have a direct Revenue Growth FWD filter, does not show Total Cash and Total Debt as standalone dollar amounts, and uses a slightly different PEG calculation. These limitations are why Finviz is a starting point, not a final answer. Every company the Finviz screener surfaces should be verified against all 10 metrics in Seeking Alpha before adding it to the watchlist.

- [ ] 65. **h2** [main]: Step 2: Set the Filters
- [ ] 66. **paragraph** [main]: The table below shows the recommended filter settings for surfacing candidates that align with this methodology. Set each filter using the dropdown menus in the screener. You do not need to set all of them at once. Start with the most important filters (PEG, EPS Growth) and add more to narrow results if needed. The two technical filters at the end are optional context filters: they surface beaten-down candidates worth a fundamental look, but they are never buy criteria in this methodology.

- [ ] 69. **table row** [main]: Market Cap \| Descriptors \| Mid or Large \| Filters out micro/nano caps that lack analyst coverage and liquidity

- [ ] 74. **table row** [main]: Sales Growth Qtr/Qtr \| Fundamental \| Over 10% \| Revenue momentum proxy. Finviz does not have a direct Revenue FWD filter; QoQ growth confirms current top-line expansion
