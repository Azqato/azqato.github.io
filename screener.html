Scoring Methodology

Each stock is graded relative to the rest of the Nasdaq 100 on five forward-looking metrics. For each metric a stock earns 0 to 20 points based on where it ranks against its peers, and the five add up to a Score out of 100. This is a ranking, not a fixed-threshold test: a high score means a stock looks better than most of the index right now, not that it cleared a specific target.

PASS 80–100 WATCH 50–79 FAIL below 50

The five metrics

MetricBetter means
Revenue Growth FWDhigher (faster sales growth)
EPS Growth FWDhigher (faster profit growth)
P/E FWD vs EPS Growthlower (cheaper for the growth)
PEG FWDlower (cheaper for the growth)
Cash vs Debthigher (more cash than debt)

A company with negative forward earnings (a negative P/E) ranks worst on both P/E vs Growth and PEG, not best, since "cheap relative to earnings" has no meaning when there are no earnings. For those names the PEG column shows a negative value (computed from forward P/E and growth) rather than the data provider's misleading positive figure. A company whose forward earnings are expected to shrink (EPS growth at or below 0) likewise ranks worst on P/E vs Growth.

How a rank becomes points

For each metric, every stock is ranked against the others and given a percentile: the share of peers it beats (for "lower is better" metrics, being cheaper counts as beating). The percentile maps to points on this curve.

Where it ranksPoints
Bottom 25% (0 to 25th percentile)0
40th percentile6
Median (50th percentile)10
60th percentile14
Top 25% (75th percentile and up)20

Formula: points = 40 × (percentile - 0.25), capped between 0 and 20. The middle half does the sliding; the top and bottom quarters are flat. The table's cell colors follow the same ranking: a green cell is top-quartile on that metric, red is bottom-quartile, and amber is in between.

Worked example: Apple's PEG

Apple's PEG of about 2.3 is cheaper than roughly 29% of the index. Because 29% sits just above the bottom quarter, that earns about 1.7 of 20 on the PEG metric. Not because 2.3 fails a fixed bar, but because almost every other Nasdaq 100 name is cheaper. A stock with a PEG near the cheapest in the group (top 25%) would earn the full 20.

Adding up and the Factors chip

A stock that is exactly average on all five metrics scores 5 × 10 = 50. The Factors column (for example 3/5) counts how many metrics land in the upper part of the pack (15+ of 20, roughly the top third). If a metric is unavailable, it is dropped and the score is rescaled across the rest.

Good to know

Because grades are relative, a stock's score can shift when other companies' numbers change, even if its own do not, and by design roughly the bottom third of the index always lands in Fail. Scores are computed across whichever stocks are currently loaded (normally the full Nasdaq 100 from the daily feed). Forward figures track Seeking Alpha; EPS Growth FWD is GAAP-basis (the * note). The screener grades fundamentals only: technical signals like RSI and the 52-week range are not part of any stock's score, because in this methodology they are used to time index and ETF purchases, not to judge individual companies.
